recorded under section crpc therefore there was no benchmark against which to test whether the appellants who he had identified after over a year of the crime and who he had fleetingly seen were in is never held to be corroboratory evidence to identify the culprit another piece of evidence on the basis of which the conviction of the appellant was recorded is the recovery of kalashnikov p along with three live bullets allegedly effected from a ghair abad dhari abandoned house and taken into possession vide recovery memo exhpj to prove the said recovery the prosecution produced the attesting witnesses of exhpj namely ghulam sabir pw and abdul salam pw however the said recovery is totally inconsequential in proving the guilt of the appellant for murder reference no of criminal appeal no of multiple reasons firstly no crime empty was sent to the punjab forensic science agency pfsa for matching with the recovered kalashnikov p the absence of such forensic linkage renders the recovery a disjointed event unconnected with the actual offence in question it is now firmly established that mere recovery of a weapon cannot by itself be treated as incriminating unless it is corroborated through forensic comparison with crime empties reliance in this regard is placed on the case titled nasir ahmed vs the state scmr wherein it was held that in the absence of forensic examination linking the recovered weapon of offence the recovery of the same cannot be given probative value secondly the prosecution failed to prove that the abandoned house from which the kalashnikov allegedly recovered was in the exclusive possession of the appellant the site plan prepared by the investigating officer did not establish such exclusive dominion nor was there any other circumstantial evidence to connect the place of recovery with the appellant personally the principle that recovery must be from the conscious and exclusive possession of the accused has been emphasized by the august supreme court of pakistan in case of arshad khan vs the state scmr wherein it is held that recoveries effected from open or unoccupied places or from places not shown to be in exclusive possession of the accused carry little evidentiary value thirdly the recovery was witnessed and attested only by prosecution witnesses who were related to the complainant and deceased therefore the recovery of kalashnikov p along with three live bullets neither meets the requirements of credibility nor fulfills the legal criteria of relevance the learned trial court erred in giving undue significance to this piece of evidence while convicting the appellant the prosecution in our considered view has miserably failed to prove the guilt of the appellant beyond reasonable doubt as repeatedly enunciated by the superior courts in a chain of cases including tariq pervez v the state scmr ayub masih v the state pld sc and sikandar ali alias bhola vs the state scmr the benefit of doubt however slight arising from any infirmity in the prosecution case must always go to the accused it is not simply a procedural requirement murder reference no of criminal appeal no of rather it serves as a crucial protection within the criminal justice system that helps preventing wrongful convictions resultantly we allow criminal appeal no of setaside the conviction sentences awarded to shahid mahmood alias
