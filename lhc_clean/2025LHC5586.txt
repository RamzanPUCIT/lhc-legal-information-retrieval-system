consequently the contention that the substitution of subsection and the omission of sub section a have obliterated the jurisdiction to constitute single benches is misconceived subsection of section c continues to operate as an independent enabling provision which empowers the chairman or any duly authorized member to sit singly and hear cases allocated to the bench subject to pecuniary limits this provision operates ex proprio vigore and is not contingent upon the existence or constitution of a twomember bench it is a settled canon of statutory interpretation that where the legislature intends to curtail or withdraw an existing jurisdiction such intent must be manifested through express words or necessary implication the mere omission of subsection a does not by itself extinguish the distinct jurisdiction already preserved under subsection rather the retention of subsection after the amendment demonstrates the legislative intent to preserve the jurisdiction of single benches within the specified pecuniary limits the principle expressio unius est exclusio alterius does not apply in the present context because the legislature while omitting subsection a consciously restructured subsection and retained subsection as a substantive and independent basis of jurisdiction in view of the foregoing discussion the orders passed by the single member of the tribunal both
